# Sources and software notices

This application uses original SPHEREx L2 archive data served by NASA/IPAC IRSA. IMAGE is calibrated surface brightness, not an AI-generated image. Each observation and calibration actually used retains its original bytes and full SHA256 in data/manifests/datasets.json and the corresponding comparison/evidence export. Source URLs, release, observation times, calibration and processing limitations are preserved. Archive access and data use do not imply NASA endorsement or permission to use institutional logos. No NASA logo assets are included.

The catalog proper-motion prediction uses SIMBAD ε Indi B ICRS J2000 coordinates and proper motions, with the original URL/bibcode and omitted terms recorded in science/spherex.py and evidence. It is not a measured centroid or a validated motion detection. DEV-002 also uses Pluto S12/S14 and calibration L051, tied to observer-specific JPL Horizons predictions for the SPHEREx spacecraft. Its TLE-based trajectory and source-association limits are retained.

The universe scene uses preserved [JPL Horizons](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/horizons/) geometric heliocentric states and the [JPL physical parameters table](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/planets/phys_par.html). Exact response hashes, API parameters, epochs, frames, mean radii and uncertainties are in data/manifests/solar_system_ephemeris.json. The four acquired UTC epochs remain immutable source knots. DEV-003 adds explicitly labelled cubic Hermite display interpolation within their 72-hour range, using AU positions and AU/TDB-day velocities while showing UTC. It does not replace original ephemerides or observations. A separate data/manifests/pluto_ephemeris.json preserves the spacecraft-specific exposure predictions and DataLink calibration checks. The display camera is not the SPHEREx observer. Distance compression, enlarged bodies, fallback colors, fill lighting and decorative star points are visual representations; the star points are not a catalog. DEV-004's sourced surface maps are distinguished from procedural illustrative appearances. Three.js is MIT-licensed, with its installed notice included in the release license index.

The nine orbit lines use preserved JPL Horizons osculating elements in data/manifests/solar_system_orbits.json. UTC scene epochs are converted to TDB for ELEMENTS queries. Each closed line is a Kepler guide to the instantaneous osculating orbit, not a centuries-long integrated trajectory or a measurement. The Sun is the heliocentric origin and no orbit is invented for a stellar direction marker. See the official [osculating element definitions](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/horizons/manual.html#osculating-orbital-elements-table) and [Horizons API time types](https://ssd-api.jpl.nasa.gov/doc/horizons.html).

During continuous playback the nine guides are pinned to 2026-11-14 00:00 UTC and labelled separately from the changing model epoch. data/manifests/timeline_validation.json preserves 27 separately queried JPL midpoint states and their response hashes. The maximum observed interpolation discrepancy at those samples is about 18.99 km; this is not a global error bound, an uncertainty estimate or an independent dynamical-model validation. Playback does not change SPHEREx exposure timestamps, images, observers or scientific judgments. The interpolation and playback implementation was assisted by Codex and is recorded as DEV-003 in the AI usage and work logs.

DEV-004 retains the [JPL satellite discovery catalog](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/sats/discovery.html), [mean-element table](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/sats/elem/) and [physical parameters](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/sats/phys_par/) as separate sources. Mean elements only inform membership and sampling cadence; actual displayed states require saved Horizons vectors. Additional TNO moon membership uses NASA's [Haumea](https://science.nasa.gov/dwarf-planets/haumea/), [Eris](https://science.nasa.gov/dwarf-planets/eris/) and [Makemake](https://science.nasa.gov/dwarf-planets/makemake/) descriptions, Fraser & Brown's [Quaoar/Weywot study](https://doi.org/10.1088/0004-637X/714/2/1547), Brown & Butler's [Vanth/Dysnomia study](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-3881/aad9f2), and Arakawa et al.'s [Gonggong/Xiangliu study](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-3881/ac1f91). The manifest preserves citations and identity responses without treating published historical orbits as new 2026 observations. Five TNO moons use the same precomputed PRIMARY-body solution as their parents; earlier system-barycenter files and source-reported limitations are retained. Unavailable satellites have no invented location.

The 31 adopted maps retain exact published file bytes, source links, observation dates, map-window UV bounds, credits and unobserved-area limitations in data/manifests/appearance.json. Ring center-distance radii come from NASA/PDS and NASA planetary factsheets. Pole/rotation parameters come from the unmodified [NAIF pck00011.tpc](https://naif.jpl.nasa.gov/pub/naif/generic_kernels/pck/pck00011.tpc), with per-object source and epoch retained. The IAU pole model does not establish registered texture longitude or a measured spin phase; Earth's model is not a high-precision EOP orientation. Circular ring envelopes, opacity, narrow-ring centerline guides and enlarged dimensions are display choices. Original map files are not cropped or resized locally; map windows are selected by GPU UV coordinates.

The dated [NASA Science public exoplanet catalog](https://science.nasa.gov/exoplanets/exoplanet-catalog/) snapshot contains 6,470 planets and 4,862 host systems from 115 preserved official REST pages. It is distinct from an Exoplanet Archive PS default-solution query, which was not acquired successfully in this run. Each record links to its NASA source and preserves reported estimates and missing values. Direction markers do not assert a validated distance or current planet position. Classification-based GLSL terrains/clouds and schematic systems are code-rendered illustrations, not resolved exoplanet maps, observations or existence-probability results. Their orientation, phase and assumed 24-hour spin are disclosed separately from a reported orbital-period playback option.

Application code and translations were assisted by Codex and collaborating agents. Actual checks and remaining human review are recorded in docs/ai_usage.md and docs/worklog.md. Japanese, Traditional Chinese and French have not received native-speaker review. No generated image/video/audio replaces observations.

DEV-005's conceptual chronology preserves four official source documents: [NASA Webb's Early Universe](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/early-universe/), [ESA Cosmic eras](https://www.esa.int/articles/about-us/directorates/science/cosmic-eras), [NASA's first-star explainer](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/science-overview/science-explainers/what-were-the-first-stars-like/) and [NASA Solar System Facts](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/solar-system-facts/). data/manifests/cosmic_history.json records source hashes, approximate anchors/era spans and differences between educational descriptions. The adopted rounded 13.8-Gyr present-age reference and lookback subtraction are not a new cosmological fit. Procedural plasma/CMB, star/galaxy/filament and Solar System renderings are conceptual illustrations; no source observation image or current catalog position is replayed into the past. Display weights, point counts and expansion scale are not measured densities or a physical radiation/expansion model.

## DEV-007 published early-universe observations

The current cosmic-history viewer displays five unmodified published observation images: Cosmic Gems, Firefly Sparkle, CEERS observed cutouts, REBELS-25 ALMA maps, and ZS7. These are processed observational products, not raw detector images, natural-color surface maps, generated images or reconstructions of galaxy evolution. Source-image SHA256/dimensions, full credits, processing and lensing notes, source pages and usage-policy references are recorded in data/manifests/cosmic_observations.json and Data_Spec.md. Full credit and the applicable license/use-policy link appear next to each image.

ESA/Webb and ESO images retain CC-BY-4.0 attribution requirements; NASA image records cite the applicable NASA media-use guidance and do not assign an invented CC license. Agency endorsement is not implied. Existing conceptual history code remains archival and is not imported by the current App. Images are rendered with contain sizing; their source bytes, annotations and credits are not altered.
