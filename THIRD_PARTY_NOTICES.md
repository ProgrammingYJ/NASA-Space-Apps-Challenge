# Sources and software notices

This application uses original SPHEREx L2 archive data served by NASA/IPAC IRSA. IMAGE is calibrated surface brightness, not an AI-generated image. Each observation and calibration actually used retains its original bytes and full SHA256 in data/manifests/datasets.json and the corresponding comparison/evidence export. Source URLs, release, observation times, calibration and processing limitations are preserved. Archive access and data use do not imply NASA endorsement or permission to use institutional logos. No NASA logo assets are included.

The catalog proper-motion prediction uses SIMBAD ε Indi B ICRS J2000 coordinates and proper motions, with the original URL/bibcode and omitted terms recorded in science/spherex.py and evidence. It is not a measured centroid or a validated motion detection. DEV-002 also uses Pluto S12/S14 and calibration L051, tied to observer-specific JPL Horizons predictions for the SPHEREx spacecraft. Its TLE-based trajectory and source-association limits are retained.

The universe scene uses preserved [JPL Horizons](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/horizons/) geometric heliocentric states and the [JPL physical parameters table](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/planets/phys_par.html). Exact response hashes, API parameters, epochs, frames, mean radii and uncertainties are in data/manifests/solar_system_ephemeris.json. The four acquired UTC epochs remain immutable source knots. DEV-003 adds explicitly labelled cubic Hermite display interpolation within their 72-hour range, using AU positions and AU/TDB-day velocities while showing UTC. It does not replace original ephemerides or observations. A separate data/manifests/pluto_ephemeris.json preserves the spacecraft-specific exposure predictions and DataLink calibration checks. The display camera is not the SPHEREx observer. Distance compression, enlarged bodies, colors, fill lighting and decorative star points are visual representations; the star points are not a catalog, and planet appearances are not observations. Three.js is MIT-licensed, with its installed notice included in the release license index.

The nine orbit lines use preserved JPL Horizons osculating elements in data/manifests/solar_system_orbits.json. UTC scene epochs are converted to TDB for ELEMENTS queries. Each closed line is a Kepler guide to the instantaneous osculating orbit, not a centuries-long integrated trajectory or a measurement. The Sun is the heliocentric origin and no orbit is invented for a stellar direction marker. See the official [osculating element definitions](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/horizons/manual.html#osculating-orbital-elements-table) and [Horizons API time types](https://ssd-api.jpl.nasa.gov/doc/horizons.html).

During continuous playback the nine guides are pinned to 2026-11-14 00:00 UTC and labelled separately from the changing model epoch. data/manifests/timeline_validation.json preserves 27 separately queried JPL midpoint states and their response hashes. The maximum observed interpolation discrepancy at those samples is about 18.99 km; this is not a global error bound, an uncertainty estimate or an independent dynamical-model validation. Playback does not change SPHEREx exposure timestamps, images, observers or scientific judgments. The interpolation and playback implementation was assisted by Codex and is recorded as DEV-003 in the AI usage and work logs.

Application code and translations were assisted by Codex and collaborating agents. Actual checks and remaining human review are recorded in docs/ai_usage.md and docs/worklog.md. Japanese, Traditional Chinese and French have not received native-speaker review. No generated image/video/audio replaces observations.
